# Employee Stock Options (ESOP) and Vesting

This article provides an overview of Employee Stock Options (ESOP) at NovaTech Solutions, including details on grants, the vesting schedule, exercising options, implications of leaving the company, and tax considerations. It also explains how to manage your ESOP through the EquityPortal.

## Grants

At NovaTech Solutions, Employee Stock Options (ESOPs) are granted to eligible employees as part of their compensation package. The grant is typically communicated through an official notification, which includes details such as the number of options granted and the grant date.

## Vesting Schedule and Cliff

ESOPs at NovaTech Solutions follow a 4-year vesting schedule with a 1-year cliff. This means:

1. **1-Year Cliff**: You must remain employed for one full year before any options vest. After this period, 25% of your granted options will vest.
2. **Quarterly Vesting**: After the cliff, the remaining 75% of your options will vest quarterly over the next three years. This results in 6.25% of the options vesting every quarter.

## Exercising Options

Once your options have vested, you can exercise them. To exercise your options:

1. Log in to the EquityPortal at [EquityPortal](https://equity.novatech.example).
2. Navigate to the 'My Options' section to view your vested options.
3. Select the options you wish to exercise and follow the prompts to complete the transaction.

Note: You will need to provide payment for the exercise price, which is typically set at the fair market value on the grant date.

## Leaving the Company

If you leave NovaTech Solutions, the treatment of your unexercised options will depend on the circumstances of your departure:

- **Voluntary Resignation**: You typically have 90 days from your last working day to exercise any vested options. Unvested options will be forfeited.
- **Termination for Cause**: All options, both vested and unvested, will be forfeited immediately.
- **Termination without Cause**: Similar to voluntary resignation, you will have 90 days to exercise your vested options, while unvested options will be forfeited.

## Tax at Exercise

Exercising your options may have tax implications. The difference between the exercise price and the fair market value of the shares at the time of exercise is considered taxable income. It is advisable to consult with a tax professional to understand your specific tax obligations.
